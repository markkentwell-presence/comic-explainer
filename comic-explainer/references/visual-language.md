# Visual language

Adapted from Ian Xiaohei Illustrations by Ian (@ianneo_ai), MIT.

Make a serious idea visible through an unusual but understandable physical action. A creature squeezing damp soil over an empty cup explains the gap between interest and a usable result. It is the action, not decoration, that carries the meaning.

## Defaults

- 16:9 horizontal, one standalone scene per file.
- Pure white background. No cream paper, texture, gradients or decorative shadows.
- Lightly wobbly black pen linework. Keep at least roughly a third of the canvas quiet.
- Black linework and main text; orange for movement; blue for state or supporting explanation; red for a problem or warning. Use fewer colours when enough.
- Three to six short, exact labels. Avoid paragraphs, title boxes and unreadable microtext.
- Recurring Xiaohei: irregular solid-black body, two white dot eyes, thin limbs, blank expression. Deadpan rather than cute. Actively digging, opening, measuring, carrying, repairing or testing.

## Fresh metaphors

Convert the concept to an action, choose one or two simple physical objects, then give the character a meaningful job. A bottleneck can become a narrow doorway, a hidden barrier a locked gate, feedback a tray of collected clues. The best metaphor reveals the causal relationship. It should not need a long explanation to make the geometry plausible.

Do not copy an upstream example's composition merely because the topic resembles it. Keep the style, invent the current scene. Maintain visual continuity across a sequence by carrying forward the character, a prop or the result of the previous action.

## Prompt skeleton

```text
Label everything in English.
Generate one standalone 16:9 horizontal hand-drawn comic explainer scene.
Pure white background. Minimal black pen linework with slight wobble.
At least 35% quiet white space. Mature, deadpan, lightly absurd visual humour.
Recurring character: an irregular solid-black creature, two white dot eyes,
thin limbs, blank serious expression. It must perform the central action.

Core idea: [one sentence]
Action and composition: [the physical action and the relationship it reveals]
Exact handwritten labels: [three to six short labels, with colours]

Black: main lines and text. Orange: movement only. Blue: state only.
Red: problems only. Do not add extra labels or a title.
Do not write directional words such as down, up, left or right on the image.
No dense infographic, formal flowchart, poster, grid or childish mascot.
```

Spatial descriptions belong in ordinary prose, not shouting directions. For geometry that matters, specify the relationship directly: “the two connected water surfaces share the same horizontal level.” Check the actual render afterwards.

## Targeted edit example

“Change only the three tally groups beneath ‘20 inspections’ to four groups of five, totalling twenty. Preserve all other labels, characters, positions, linework and colours.”

Do not repeatedly edit a confused layout. Rewrite the core spatial relationship and regenerate when needed.
